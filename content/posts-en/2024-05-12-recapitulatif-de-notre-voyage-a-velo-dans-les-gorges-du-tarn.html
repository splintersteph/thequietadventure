For anyone who might be interested, for families who feel inspired, we've gathered information on this page so you can follow our route. We'd rate this trip as difficult; you need strong thighs to climb the peaks that await you. But go for it without fear: if you take your time, anything is possible!

To read the whole adventure in detail, you'll find the first part between Gaillac and Ambialet, the second between Ambialet and Saint-Rome-de-Tarn and the last one between Saint-Rome-de-Tarn and Génolhac.

Gear

  • Here's the gear we used:
    • A light, sturdy Kona Rove gravel bike; it weighs only 11 kilos and pulls the trailer.
    • A Thule Chariot Cross Alaska single-child trailer
    • The other bike is a Decathlon Riverside Touring 920; it weighs 18 kilos and carries most of the weight in its panniers.
    • A Shotgun seat so Clémence can ride up front with a panoramic view.
    • Komoot, to plan routes and guide us live.

The route

Stage 1: Saturday 4 May 2024

We slept in an Airbnb in Toulouse (€70) to start the route by train. After 40 minutes on the train, we were in Gaillac (€3 for the three of us, as it was the first weekend of the month). From Gaillac we rode to Albi, where we stayed with friends.

On the bike: 23 kilometres in 1 h 27, 88 metres of climbing.

Stage 2: Sunday 5 May 2024

We slept in Ambialet in a B&B with breakfast included for €70.

On the bike: 35 kilometres in 2 h 15 and 213 metres of climbing.

Stage 3: Monday 6 May 2024

We slept at the Relays du Chasteau in Brousse-le-Château for €60, breakfast included.

On the bike: 33 kilometres in 2 hours and 200 metres of climbing.

Stage 4: Tuesday 7 May 2024

We slept at Camping La Cascade in Saint-Rome-de-Tarn for €60.

On the bike: 37 kilometres in 3 hours and 700 metres of climbing.

Stage 5: Wednesday 8 May 2024

We slept at the Moulin de Pareyre in Les Vignes for €100, breakfast included.

On the bike: 61 kilometres in 4 h 30 and almost 500 metres of climbing.

Stage 6: Thursday 9 May 2024

We slept at Hôtel Burlatis for €70 with a very generous breakfast included.

On the bike: our GPS malfunctioned that day and merged two rides into one. That Thursday, we rode 30 kilometres in 2 hours with 300 metres of climbing.

Stage 7: Friday 10 May 2024

We slept at Camping Chantemerle in a little cabin for €54; the breakfast is not recommended.

On the bike: our GPS malfunctioned that day too and merged two rides into one. That Friday, we rode 30 kilometres in 2 hours with 300 metres of climbing.

Stage 8: Saturday 11 May 2024

We splashed out a little by staying at the Mas Nouveau in Génolhac. For €120 we spent a night somewhere slightly out of time. Breakfast was included and offered endless choice.

On the bike: this was the hardest day. We rode 45 kilometres in 3 h 40 with 825 metres of climbing.
