Sunday 16 August

For Cassandre's thirtieth birthday we rent two bikes and ride the superb paved road through the Bow Valley, closed to cars all summer. Canada hasn't accustomed us to such consideration for cyclists, and we relish the chance with undisguised pleasure. We stop at every viewpoint and use the signal to call our families, first to thank them for the messages Cass received, then to show them the fabulous panorama before us. Twenty-five kilometres of ups and downs bring us to the finish, the start of the day's walk along Johnston Canyon. Words are starting to repeat themselves and no longer do justice to the beauty of this majestic place.

We're storing up memories for years, and in the evening we want to celebrate Cassandre's birthday at a restaurant in Banff. No luck: they all close very early, and those still open announce a long wait, the town being packed on this mid-August weekend. Never mind: a trip to the supermarket, a bottle and a few treats, and we toast on the patio of our B&B, eyes on the mountains. Despite the disappointment of the missed restaurant, it's an excellent evening, slightly tipsy and full of laughter.

Monday 17 August

We splashed out on three nights in a hotel, but Cass's thirtieth was worth the luxury. With rooms at 150 dollars minimum, though, we can't spend our whole stay in the Rockies that way: ten days of roughing it await. So this morning we linger in bed before checking out at 11, and saying goodbye to our hostess, we know we're also saying goodbye to comfort for a good while. After two days that were hard on the muscles, we plan something more relaxed: a three-hour hike up and down Sulphur Mountain, five minutes' drive away, then lazing in the sun.

In our seasoned hikers' gear we tackle an hour and a half of climbing, with fine views over the valley, only to arrive… at the top station of the gondola. It's a little frustrating to reach the summit drenched in sweat when the cabins disgorge tourists in flip-flops every five minutes. This peak is an aberration: the gondola building houses a huge complex, restaurants and Starbucks included. Canadians have a very capitalist approach to nature and don't hesitate to make it pay: the round trip costs sixty dollars for those who can afford it and can't be bothered to walk. Every tree along the ridge has been cut down to make a perfectly smooth boardwalk leading in ten minutes to the neighbouring summit. The place has a touch of Disneyland, and we feel out of place among these tourists in sandals, Starbucks cup in hand, snapping photos at every turn. Thoroughly put off by this ecological massacre, we don't linger and walk back down. We pass a few hikers, very few compared with the crowd the gondola delivers.

To wash, we think of going to the lake, but every car park is full, and the park strictly forbids parking by the road. If the car park is full, all you can do is turn round. We spot a small river with clear water and a strip of earth beside it. We park there and walk down to the bank through tall grass. The sun is beating down, and just as well, because the water, coming straight from the glacier, is under ten degrees. It's in this icy water that we wash, not without difficulty. The spot is pleasant, out of sight, and we spend a few hours there before heading back to town. This time we book a table as soon as the restaurant we wanted to try opens, and we can really celebrate Cassandre's birthday. On a pretty terrace we savour finely cooked dishes, before finding a discreet corner of town for the night.

Tuesday 18 August

No rush this morning: the hike we have planned doesn't draw crowds. Away from town, the Healy Pass trail is too demanding for the city folk who haunt Banff, and we arrive around 9:30. Seventeen kilometres and nearly a thousand metres of climbing: enough to keep us busy. We set off, as always, with music to warn bears of our presence. The first six kilometres climb through the woods, then we come out onto alpine meadows studded with flowers, each more colourful than the last. The trail ends at a pass from which you take in the sweep of the Rockies and lakes as far as the eye can see. Marmot heaven, we think, and sure enough we spot a few over lunch.

The descent, faster of course, still feels long. It's hotter than this morning, the insects have come out of hiding, and the walking is less pleasant. At the car park, a surprise: mountain goats grazing not far from the car. Nature has already spoiled us today, squirrels, marmots, but the best is yet to come. On the mountain road back to the Trans-Canada we come face to face with a bear! It calmly crosses the road a few metres from the car. We stop to watch; it goes down onto the verge, where it has spotted wild berries, and starts to eat, less than five metres from us. Spellbound, silent so as not to scare it, we watch for long minutes. Our Canadian experience is starting to feel truly complete!

Still buzzing, we go back to Banff to shop for several days, since we're not sure we'll find affordable supermarkets again. Then an hour's drive to Lake Louise. This little village, or rather this string of tourist hotels, has one of the most beautiful lakes in the world, and we intend to be in the front row tomorrow morning, because we've been warned: the huge car parks are full by 8 am. We park in one of the few side streets available and sleep a well-earned sleep.
